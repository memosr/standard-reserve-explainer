01

The problem

An earlier generation of reserve protocols paid fixed emissions no matter what the market wanted. Demand went up, demand went down. The issuance schedule never noticed.

02

One signal

Net ETH flow through the pool replaces the guesswork. Buying pressure means expansion. Selling pressure means contraction. One number, read continuously.

0 contraction

net flow = ETH in minus ETH out, measured at the pool

03

The currency

$STANDARD caps at 1,000,000,000. 100,000,000 mint at genesis as protocol-owned liquidity, locked forever. The remaining 900,000,000 is the entire issuance budget. When it's gone, base issuance stops for good.

04

The charter

1,000 Founding Charters at genesis. Free, soulbound, one per wallet. After that, every new charter is sold in a daily Dutch auction, priced in ETH.

05

Branches

Every charter opens with 1 branch, up to 10. Every branch beyond the first is bought by burning $STANDARD. Growth is a supply sink.

06

Issuance

Each day's issue is split pro rata across every branch in the system. Your cut is your branches divided by all branches, so the same issue spreads thinner as the system grows.

07

Regimes

One system, two faces. Net inflow: reserves stack. Net outflow: buyback and burn. The mechanism doesn't pick a side. It responds to the one signal.

08

The exit

A classic bank run empties the vault for whoever's fastest. Here it's inverted: leavers pay a resolution fee, and half of that fee is burned, half flows to whoever stayed.

09

Flywheels

Four loops, one property in common: every path a banker can take either burns $STANDARD or stacks reserves. There's no neutral move.

10

The unknown

The whitepaper carries 51 redacted fields: base issuance, the multiplier, epoch length, fee curves. The mechanics are fixed. The numbers aren't public yet.
